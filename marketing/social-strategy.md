# Elsewhere Apparel Co. — TikTok & Instagram plan

Written Nov 7, 2026. Store: elsewheresupplyco.myshopify.com. 191 print-on-demand products.

## Where we're starting from

- **The product is the joke.** Lines like "Not AI. Just Tired." and "Ships in 6–7 Business
  Days" are already tiny pieces of content. On TikTok, slogan apparel sells when the video
  acts out the feeling *first* and shows the shirt *second*. Nobody scrolls for "new tee
  drop." They stop for "POV: you said you'd be there at 7."
- **The margins are thin.** About $10–15 per tee, $15–20 per hoodie. That rules out
  buying growth with ads early on. **Organic first**: let free posts show which designs
  people respond to, then put small ad money behind the posts that already worked.
- **The calendar matters right now.** Black Friday is **Nov 27**, Cyber Monday **Nov 30**.
  Print-on-demand needs about 2–5 days to print plus shipping, so the last safe
  "arrives by Christmas" order date is likely around **Dec 10–12**. Check Printify's
  posted holiday cutoffs and use their date. That leaves about 5 weeks for the Christmas
  Collection.
- **Too many products to promote all of them.** Pick heroes (below) and repeat them. People
  need to see the same shirt several times before they buy it.

## Content pillars (what every post is)

1. **Relatable skits / POV (about 50% of posts).** Act out the line, then reveal the shirt.
   You don't need to be on camera much: a text overlay, a hand, a shot over the shoulder,
   the shirt on a hanger.
2. **The product up close (about 25%).** Close-ups of the print and fabric, a "pick my fit"
   between colors, a slow pan across the Christmas art, a gift-guide carousel.
3. **Behind the brand (about 15%).** "I started a clothing brand for people who showed up
   but didn't fully arrive." Designing a shirt, the first sample arriving, an honest
   "here's what didn't sell." Founder posts build trust faster than polished ads.
4. **Gift / deal posts (about 10%).** Gift guides, Black Friday, "last day to order for
   Christmas." Only post these when the deal or deadline is real.

## 10 ready-to-film video ideas

Each one is 7–15 seconds, opens with a text hook in the first second, and ends on the shirt.

1. **"Not AI. Just Tired."** Text: *"Coworker: did you use ChatGPT for this?"* → cut to you
   slowly turning around in the tee. Caption: *verified human, low battery.*
2. **"Currently Elsewhere."** Group chat sounds pinging. You're on the couch in the
   hoodie, staring into space. Text: *"me at every event I RSVP'd yes to."*
3. **"Do Not Perceive Me."** Walking into a store and pulling the beanie lower. Text:
   *"introvert running errands starter pack."*
4. **"Ships in 6–7 Business Days."** Text: *"Mom: when are you getting married?"* →
   reveal the shirt. Swap in "having kids" or "getting a real job" for more versions.
5. **"Odysseus Layover."** Airport or car, delayed-flight board. Text: *"he took 10 years to
   get home, I can handle a 4-hour layover."*
6. **Dear Santa, Bring Bourbon.** Pour a bourbon on the rocks, then pull back to the
   hoodie's back print. Text: *"my letter to Santa this year."* (A bar or your own house;
   don't show anyone who looks underage.)
7. **The Gift Is Me.** Walk into the family party empty-handed, turn around → back print.
   Text: *"what I'm bringing to the white elephant."*
8. **Holly Jolly Chaos.** Messy holiday kitchen / tangled lights. Text: *"the vibe at
   my house Dec 24."*
9. **Gift guide carousel (Instagram) / photo slideshow (TikTok Photo Mode).** "Gifts for the
   friend who cancels plans," "under $20," "for the dad who wants bourbon." One product
   per slide, prices on each.
10. **Founder story.** Talk to camera or voiceover with b-roll: *"I made a clothing
    brand for people who are physically present but mentally elsewhere. Here's the first
    shirt that sold."*

Post the same idea in several versions (different hook, different relative, different
sound). Repeating what works beats always inventing something new.

## Where Artlist fits (and where it doesn't)

**Good uses:**
- **Licensed music.** This matters most. A **business** TikTok/Instagram account can't legally
  use most trending songs. Use Artlist tracks or TikTok's Commercial Music Library so
  posts don't get muted or removed, especially once they become ads.
- **B-roll and atmosphere.** Snowy streets, airport boards, fireplaces, cityscapes to cut
  between your real product shots.
- **Animating your own art.** The Christmas designs are already illustrations. Subtle
  motion on them (snow falling, bourbon glinting, the bow wiggling) makes good
  hooks and product posts, and it's honest because the art *is* the product.
- **Voiceovers and sound effects** for skits and ads.
- **Backgrounds** for flyers, ad graphics and lifestyle scenes for mugs and ornaments.

**Be careful with:**
- **AI people "wearing" your shirts.** AI video and image tools usually scramble printed
  text, which is your whole product. Viewers notice, and a shirt that doesn't match what
  arrives in the mail leads to returns, bad reviews and possible ad rejections. Use
  real photos/video of real shirts for anything that shows the product being worn.
- **AI labels.** TikTok and Instagram both require realistic AI-generated content to be
  labeled. Turn on the AI label when a post contains it.
- **License tier.** Before you pay, confirm your Artlist plan covers **business / brand use
  and paid ads**, not just personal social channels.

**The most important purchase is actually samples.** Order 4–6 real pieces through
Printify (Currently Elsewhere Hoodie, Not AI tee, beanie, 2–3 Christmas hoodies or tees,
one mug). Real shirts on real people will outperform anything generated, and you'll
reuse that footage for months.

## Setup checklist (one afternoon)

- [ ] Switch TikTok and Instagram to **Business** accounts, same handle on both if possible.
- [ ] Bio: one line + link. Example: *"Clothes for people who showed up but didn't fully
      arrive. ↓ Christmas drop"* Link to the store with `?utm_source=tiktok` /
      `?utm_source=instagram` so Shopify shows which platform sends buyers.
- [ ] Install Shopify's **TikTok** app and the **Facebook & Instagram** app. They sync your
      catalog, so you can **tag products** in posts and set up the tracking pixels.
- [ ] Look into **TikTok Shop** (Printify connects to it). Its affiliate program lets
      creators make videos about your shirts for a commission, which is the biggest
      no-upfront-cost growth tool for apparel right now.
- [ ] Pin 3 posts on each profile: brand intro, best-selling item, Christmas Collection.

## Posting schedule

- **TikTok:** 1 post a day for the first 30 days (film 7–10 in one session each week).
  Early on, volume matters more than polish.
- **Instagram:** post the same videos as Reels (re-export without the TikTok
  watermark), plus 2 carousels a week (gift guides, "which color?") and daily Stories
  (polls, "new drop," reposts of customers).
- **Engagement:** spend 15 minutes a day replying to every comment, often with a *video
  reply*, which counts as a free extra post. Comment on posts in your niche (introvert
  humor, cozy fits, holiday gift guides).

## When to spend money on ads

After 2–3 weeks, look for posts with **good watch time and shares** (not just likes). Boost
those as **Spark Ads** on TikTok or **boosted Reels** on Instagram at **$5–15/day**,
with the audience set to US, 18–34, interested in humor, introvert or cozy content, and gift shopping.

Rule of thumb: if an ad costs more than **about $10 per sale on a tee** or **about $15 on a
hoodie**, you lose money. Stop that ad and try a different video. Don't raise the budget
on an ad that isn't working.

## What to track (weekly)

| Metric | Where | Good sign |
|---|---|---|
| Average watch time / % watched in full | TikTok & IG analytics | Most people stay past 3 seconds; short videos get watched to the end |
| Shares & saves | Same | Better signal than likes |
| Profile visits → link clicks | Same | Rising week over week |
| Sessions & orders by UTM source | Shopify → Analytics → Reports | Which platform (and which flyer) actually brings buyers |
| Best-selling designs | Shopify orders | Make more videos about these and fewer about the rest |

Every Sunday: keep the 3 best videos (make new versions), drop the 3 worst ideas, plan
the next 7.
